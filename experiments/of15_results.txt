OF15 results: the agent zoo on simulator v4 (discovery_v4) and v3 (discovery), through the ragrl pipeline
=========================================================================================================

Pipeline: python/ragrl on the _ragof nanobind module (VecScalpEnv), configs experiments/configs/of15_<agent>_<v4|v3>.json,
regenerate with experiments/of15_regen.sh (OF15_MARKET=v3 for v3). Per-agent reports: experiments/of15_<agent>[_v3].txt
(copies of each run's report_results.md). Runs: build-avx512/rl/runs/ (index.jsonl; ledger_usage.jsonl one level up).

Setup common to every row
- ES, 1 contract, market orders, fee $1.5, latency 1 step, 1 s decisions, 600 decisions/episode, warm-up 1050 s.
- 12 run seeds (1..12) per agent, 2000 episodes x 600 decisions = 1.2M decisions per seed (of_train's budget).
  neuralq_ref = the C++ NeuralQ nets from of_train (OF14 nets for v4, OF11 nets for v3), loaded, not retrained.
- Champion per seed on validation 700M+[0,200) every 60k decisions (neuralq_ref: of_train's own champion on
  700M+[0,40), re-scored on [40,240)); comparisons on validation 701M+[0,400) ("val" column).
- Headline declared before training: the 12-seed ensemble ("ens"). "mos" = mean of the 12 seeds' per-episode P&L.
- Sealed test, scored once per config in a fresh process, every policy on the same 300 episodes (paired):
  v4: test 901M+[0,300), family of15_v4 (size 9: 8 agents + 1 HPO slot, unused);
  v3: test 901M+[300,600), family of15_v3 (size 8: the 8 agents; the family is now full).
- CIs: 95% bootstrap over episodes (B = 10000, seed 0). Paired diffs are row minus reference on the same episodes.
  "ens-flat" equals the ensemble's own mean (flat scores 0). "NeuralQ ens" = the 12-net of_train mean-Q ensemble.
- Module _ragof 9e3731fb (snapshot build-avx512/runs/of15-e85dfe7), git e85dfe7 + the OF15 working tree, venv
  freeze b6df5bd0, for every row.

What the table says
- Every learned agent's 12-seed ensemble ends up flat or nearly flat. On v4 the rppo, dqn, qrdqn, lgbm and dagger
  ensembles place 0 orders on the sealed test; neuralq_ref (0.17 orders/ep, -0.29 [-0.65,-0.04]) and ppo_fs (0.26,
  -0.13) trade a little and lose a little. PPO is the only agent that trades every episode (2.0 orders/ep): -1.10
  [-3.72,+1.51] on v4, -0.60 [-2.04,+0.84] on v3. The individual seeds that trade (mos column) lose: v4 mos from -0.10
  (dqn) to -3.80 (neuralq_ref, 6.9 orders/ep); v3 mos from +0.02 (dqn, CI [-0.04,+0.08]) to -1.09 (ppo).
- Paired vs the 30 s delta rule every agent is "better" (+4.6 to +5.8 ticks/ep on v4, +1.3 to +2.0 on v3) only because
  the delta rule pays fees on about 6 orders/episode and loses -5.75 (v4) / -1.88 (v3). That is not an edge.
- Paired vs the NeuralQ ensemble: +0.29 [+0.04,+0.65] on v4 for every agent that stays flat (the NeuralQ ensemble
  loses 0.29/ep), about 0 on v3. No agent beats the C++ NeuralQ reference by trading better on either market.
- The best positive point estimates, lgbm v3 ensemble +0.10 [+0.00,+0.29] at 0.01 orders/ep and dagger v4 mos +0.01,
  are a handful of trades in 300 episodes; t is far below t_req (about 5.1-5.2). dagger v3 looked positive on
  validation (+0.14 ens, +0.13 mos) and went negative on test (-0.15, -0.23): validation noise, as in OF7.
- Consistent with OF11-OF14: with fees and 1 s market orders neither simulator offers an edge that RL, supervised
  learning or imitation of the privileged teacher can take; what the agents learn is to stay flat.

Simulator throughput (raw VecScalpEnv, random actions, 1 thread, same snapshot, train stream run seed 1): v4 11,200
decisions/s alone and 9,000/s with 12 in parallel; v3 24,700/s alone. So 1.2M decisions cost about 130 s (v4) or
50-60 s (v3) per worker, warm-up included. The SB3 adapter (RagVecEnv) adds < 1%; a 64x64 MLP forward over the batch
costs < 2 s per 1.2M decisions.

Is training model-bound? Mostly not.
- PPO, PPO frame-stack, recurrent PPO and all evaluation are simulator-bound on v4: 61-80% of training time in the
  C++ simulator, about 90% counting validation checkpoints. A GPU could save at most the remaining 10-20%.
- DQN (about 60% network) and QR-DQN (88-93% network: 2 gradient steps of batch 256 x 32 quantiles per env step) are
  update-bound. But the networks are 64x64 MLPs on 16 inputs, a few kFLOP per sample. At that size the time goes to
  per-update Python/SB3/launch overhead, not arithmetic, so a GPU is unlikely to help much (not measured: no GPU
  here). Cheaper levers: fewer gradient steps per env step (train_freq 4), more torch threads, more workers (24
  hardware threads, the runs used 12).
- lgbm: the non-simulator part is LightGBM fitting and labelling (CPU). dagger: behaviour-cloning fits and the
  privileged teacher (CPU, small).
Verdict: a rented GPU would not change OF15's turnaround. The levers are the simulator (v4 is about 2x slower than v3)
and the DQN-family update schedule.

5. Did the heap bug touch the sealed results?

All 16 sealed results came from the binary in use when the crashes happened: _ragof 9e3731fb is the only module OF15
ever used, and it was loaded in all 5 crashed processes. The root cause is not found and no fix shipped. "The fixed
module" is the same unchanged 9e3731fb binary. The only changes since 11-05 are on the Python side: the WMI bypass in
ragrl/__init__.py, which takes the leading suspect (Python's _wmi worker thread) out of the processes, and the R1v
edit. Why the numbers are not affected:
- 0xc0000374 is fail-fast: the heap manager detects the damage and kills the process. The crashed runs produced no
  selection or sealed output, and none of their files was reused.
- The determinism checks that would expose silent damage all pass. (a) R8: seed 1 of every run retrained from scratch
  in a fresh process, bit-identical. (b) Selection: the seed-1 champion evaluated twice and against its worker's hash,
  identical. (c) Today's audit (validation only, stored files): every sealed run's 12 champions + ensemble replayed
  in a fresh process on validation 701M+[0,400); all 16 runs x 13 policies x 3 per-episode arrays are bit-identical
  to the original select process's val_select_episodes.npz (kitlog of15_revalidate.log). (d) The sealed baselines
  (flat, delta30, 5 oracles: 21 per-episode arrays) are bit-identical across the 8 independent seal processes of each
  market. (e) The heap session: 12/12 lgbm_v4 seeds reproduced under per-step HeapValidate and 6 under ASan, with
  no report.
- Residual risk: a seal process could in principle hold damage that changed a learned policy's sealed numbers without
  crashing. Only those numbers cannot be rechecked without opening the test block again, which was never done. Heap
  damage depends on layout and timing, and every replay above is bit-exact, so this is unlikely. And every result is
  "no edge": one corrupted number could not turn that into a claim without tripping R1/R2/R3.

8. Caveats

- qrdqn v4 was sealed with --allow-env-change. The ragrl code hash changed between its training (3aa6572a) and its
  seal (6aa7b15a): the WMI bypass, needed now that WMI queries crash processes on this server, and the R1v edit.
  This is recorded in its sealed.json env_problems. Module and venv are unchanged. The seed-1 retrain under the new
  code matched bit for bit, and so did the validation replays of all 13 runs trained under the old code.
- dqn/rppo/qrdqn v3 trained under ragrl 6aa7b15a and the other 13 configs under 3aa6572a; the training code is the same.
- index.jsonl is an append-only log: it still shows RED_FLAGS for the 7 runs re-rendered with R1v. Their STATUS
  files, reports and experiments/of15_*.txt copies now say NO_EDGE.
- The full-run timings come from two days with different background load: the 11-05 v3 PPO rows are slower than
  today's profile predicts. Use the section 3 profile for the shares.
- The oracles lose after costs on v4 (as in OF14), so the "learned vs oracle" bounds are weak (R6). With 300 sealed
  episodes per market, the CIs for trading agents are about +-1.5-2.6 ticks/episode.
- Everything here is on the simulator. Nothing has been replayed on real ES ticks (ReplayScalpEnv is bound but unused).
